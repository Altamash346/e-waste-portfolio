E-Waste & E-Management

Understanding the Subject

♻️

What is E-Waste?

Electronic waste refers to discarded electrical or electronic devices — phones, computers, batteries and other equipment that have reached the end of their useful life.

🌍

Environmental Impact

Improper disposal of e-waste can release hazardous substances into soil, water and air, making responsible handling essential for environmental health.

📱

Sources of E-Waste

Common sources include mobile phones, laptops, household appliances, batteries and IT equipment that are upgraded, replaced or discarded.

🔧

Responsible Management

Proper e-waste management involves collection, segregation, safe recycling and disposal through authorized channels rather than informal handling.

♻️

Reduce, Reuse, Repair, Recycle

Extending device life through repair and reuse, and recycling responsibly when devices are no longer usable, reduces overall e-waste generation.

🌱

Sustainable Technology

Choosing durable products, supporting take-back programs and staying informed are ways technology users can act more sustainably.

Awareness

Key Concepts to Know

What counts as electronic waste?

Any device with a plug, battery or circuitry that is no longer wanted or working qualifies as e-waste.

Why responsible disposal matters

Unsafe disposal can expose handlers and the environment to hazardous materials found inside electronics.

Recycling and recovery

Authorized recyclers can recover valuable materials from old electronics while safely managing hazardous components.

Extending the life of electronics

Repairing, upgrading or donating working devices delays the point at which they become waste.

Responsible consumption

Buying only what's needed and choosing durable products reduces long-term electronic waste generation.

Digital sustainability

Sustainable technology use includes mindful upgrades, energy-conscious habits and supporting circular-economy practices.
